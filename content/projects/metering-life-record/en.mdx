problem: Every energy trading point must keep an up-to-date life record of its metering system, in an official 18-section form with hundreds of numbered fields. It was filled in on paper. Every visit, calibration or meter or modem change meant redoing it, and the history of equipment and seals was spread across documents.
responsibilities:
  - I gathered the requirements with the metering team and took the ambiguities of the official form, including its numbering errors, to a decision log for the business owners.
  - I designed the layered architecture and built the API, the client, the SQL Server data model and the document generator.
  - I integrated corporate sign-in with roles and the invitation of external users as guests.
  - I turned the company's brand manual into a design system with tokens and lint rules, and built pixel for pixel the sign-in page the project lead designed in Figma.
  - I documented 13 architecture decisions and the deployment, restore and secret rotation runbooks, and left the app in a test deployment with Docker.
constraints:
  - The official form rules. Its structure and numbering, errors included, do not change without a decision from the business owners.
  - A database shared with other company systems. The app lives only in its two schemas, with least-privilege users.
  - Nothing published is deleted. Traceability of every record is a requirement.
  - Records carry personal data of the designated person, with a right to erasure.
  - Internal users sign in with their corporate account and external operators come in as guests.
decisions:
  - title: Every edit happens inside a draft change
    rationale: Each edit lives in a draft with its change type, is reviewed with its old and new value, and publishing it stores a snapshot of the whole record with its SHA-256 fingerprint. Earlier versions never change, and two versions are compared without rebuilding anything.
    alternatives:
      - Edit the record in place and keep a per-field change history
  - title: A section registry instead of one hand-written screen per section
    rationale: Each section declares its contract, its fields and how it is assembled into the record. The assembler, the review, the completeness score and the PDF all come from that registry, so adding a section does not touch the change workflow.
    alternatives:
      - A hand-written form and endpoint for every section
  - title: The PDF is HTML printed by an offline Chromium
    rationale: The template reproduces the official form in HTML, and a background job prints it with a browser that has no network and no JavaScript, so nothing a record carries can reach the internet. The Word file comes from the same structure.
    alternatives:
      - Draw the PDF by coordinates with a PDF library
  - title: What must never be deleted is denied in the database
    rationale: Insert-only tables, such as versions, changes and the audit log, deny UPDATE and DELETE to the app's user. A new table of that kind must add its permission in the same migration, and the script that copies the schema refuses to run if one is missing.
    alternatives:
      - Rely on application code alone

outcomes:
  - The app runs in a test deployment on the company network, with corporate sign-in, while the business owners validate the form's rules.
  - Every published version generates its PDF and Word file with the official form structure and numbering, and the list of metering points exports to Excel.

lessons:
  - I would have asked the business owners earlier for the decisions that block whole sections, such as the meters, instead of moving ahead on provisional decisions.
  - I would have set up a containerized database as the only test database from day one, so tests never shared data with the test deployment.
