problem: The communications team of a power generation company was organizing its first corporate forum and needed one tool for the whole event cycle, from publishing the agenda and speakers to registering and badging guests, measuring satisfaction and delivering certificates and memories.
responsibilities:
  - Designed the experience from the official graphic pieces, measuring colors and dimensions straight from the PDFs.
  - Built the SPA, the server, corporate authentication and the email sends.
  - Deployed on Linux with nginx and a hardened systemd unit, and was on call on forum day.
  - After the event, generated the certificates, published the gallery and added a digital business card module.
constraints:
  - Nine days between the first commit and forum day.
  - Hundreds of attendees signing in at once from the same corporate network.
  - Minimal personal data, because the server was not supposed to know names or ID numbers.
  - One person in charge of development, deployment and operations.
decisions:
  - title: Corporate sign-in with OIDC and PKCE
    rationale: Guests already had an account in the company directory. Microsoft Entra ID handled identity and roles without new passwords, and Microsoft Graph sent the emails from the institutional mailbox.
    alternatives:
      - A custom sign-up with email and password
  - title: The server only knows opaque identifiers
    rationale: Certificates and downloads are served through identifier-to-file manifests generated outside the server. Nobody can request someone else's certificate because the route takes no parameters.
    alternatives:
      - Store names and ID numbers in the server database
  - title: The survey unlocks on the server clock
    rationale: The satisfaction survey link only reaches the browser once the server clock passes the closing time. Nobody can answer it before the forum ends.
    alternatives:
      - Hide it behind a timer in the browser
  - title: Bulk send enabled only by two variables
    rationale: The bulk send has a dry-run mode, a recipient cap and resume support, and opening it to every guest requires changing two variables at once.
    alternatives:
      - A script that sends straight to the whole list

outcomes:
  - The system ran live on forum day without storing personal data on the server.
  - Each of the 163 QR codes was decoded before sending, and the send happened exactly once.
  - 156 certificates and 77 photos were published, with streamed downloads of the event memories (the largest package was 1.33 GB).

lessons:
  - Before the event I would load-test with simulated traffic from a single IP. Capacity was sized with calculations only.
  - I would separate from the start what had to outlive the event, such as the business cards, from what was ephemeral.
