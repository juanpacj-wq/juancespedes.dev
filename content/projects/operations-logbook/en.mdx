problem: Operations staff at two thermal power plants kept their shift logbooks in Excel workbooks. Information was scattered, closing a shift had no guarantees, and official reports were assembled by hand.
constraints:
  - Users on 24-hour shifts whose operating day crosses midnight.
  - Mandatory corporate single sign-on with role-based permissions.
  - Self-hosted on the corporate network, behind a firewall with TLS inspection.
  - Minimal third-party dependencies to keep auditing and maintenance simple.
decisions:
  - title: Shifts as a business entity, closed atomically
    rationale: Closing a shift freezes the team roster into an immutable snapshot, so history does not change when staffing changes later.
    alternatives:
      - Log loose events and rebuild shifts at query time
  - title: Real time over WebSockets with origin validation
    rationale: Several people work on the same shift at once; changes arrive instantly, and origin validation prevents connection hijacking.
    alternatives:
      - Poll the server every few seconds
  - title: In-house .xlsx reader and writer on top of zlib
    rationale: The official report had to match the Excel template exactly without adding large libraries to the backend.
    alternatives:
      - Use a third-party spreadsheet library
lessons:
  - Tests that guard dangerous invariants (never delete a real plant's data) are worth more than coverage on its own.
